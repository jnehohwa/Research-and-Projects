# Delimitation of Study - Joshua's Working Sheet

## What this section must do

The delimitation explains the boundaries deliberately chosen for the study. It tells the reader who and what the research covers, how the information will be collected, and what falls outside the study.

This section is worth 5 marks, so it should be focused and concise. It should not repeat the full problem statement, research methodology or benefits of the study.

## Confirmed facts from the proposal

Use the facts below when writing the section in your own words.

### Participants

- The study focuses on software engineering students.
- Participants must have previously used AI-powered coding assistants.
- The proposal does not specify a sample size, campus, geographical area or year of study. Do not add these details unless the group confirms them and updates the proposal consistently.

### Research focus

- The study investigates students' use and perceptions of AI-powered coding assistants.
- It focuses on perceived coding productivity and perceived code quality.
- Examples of relevant tools in the proposal include ChatGPT, GitHub Copilot and DeepSeek.

### Data collection

- The study uses a quantitative survey design.
- Data will be collected through an anonymous online questionnaire.
- Most questions will use five-point agreement or frequency scales.
- Participation will be limited to students with relevant experience using AI coding assistants.

### Areas covered by the questionnaire

- Whether students believe AI speeds up their programming progress.
- Whether students believe they learn from using AI.
- How often AI gives a correct answer from a single prompt.
- How often students are satisfied with AI-generated code quality.
- How often students use inline code completion, AI chat platforms and AI agents in an integrated development environment.

### Analysis boundary

- Responses will be analysed in Microsoft Excel.
- The analysis will use descriptive statistics such as frequencies, percentages, averages and response distributions.
- Results may be presented with tables, bar graphs and pie charts.

### What falls outside the study

- The study does not directly test participants while they complete programming tasks.
- It does not independently inspect or grade the source code produced by participants.
- It does not directly measure actual task-completion time or actual code quality.
- It does not prove that using AI causes improved productivity or code quality.
- Its findings are mainly based on participants' reported experiences and perceptions.

## Writing prompts

Answer these prompts in your own words before combining the answers into one short paragraph:

1. Which specific group of people is included in the study?
2. What previous experience must a participant have?
3. Which two main effects of AI coding assistants does the study examine?
4. How will the researchers collect the participants' responses?
5. What kind of information will the questionnaire measure?
6. What will the study not measure directly?
7. Why should the findings be described as perceptions rather than proof of cause and effect?

## Suggested paragraph order

Use this sequence when writing your paragraph:

1. **Opening boundary:** Identify the participant group and required AI-tool experience.
2. **Topic boundary:** State that the research focuses on students' perceptions of coding productivity and code quality.
3. **Method boundary:** Briefly identify the anonymous quantitative questionnaire and five-point scales.
4. **Exclusion:** Explain that actual programming performance and source-code quality will not be tested directly.
5. **Interpretation boundary:** State that the findings represent self-reported perceptions and cannot establish cause and effect.

Do not copy these labels into the final group document. They are only a guide for structuring your paragraph.
